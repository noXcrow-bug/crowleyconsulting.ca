Suite turnover checklist

Before a turnover crew starts in a pre-1990 suite, check whether the work will disturb any of these. If it will and you don't know what's in them, stop and test first.

Take the Obligation Check
  • 9-inch floor tile and its masticCommon in older suites and hallways; lifting or grinding it disturbs both layers.
  • Drywall joint compound and plasterSanding, cutting or demolishing walls releases it.
  • Stipple and textured ceilingsScraping or sanding a textured ceiling is a classic exposure.
  • Pipe lagging and boiler insulationOften in basements, risers and mechanical rooms.
  • Vermiculite insulationLoose, pebble-like fill in attics and wall cavities.
  • Lead paintSanding or heat-stripping old painted trim, doors and windows.
  • Mercury thermostatsOlder dial thermostats contain mercury; remove them intact.
  • Tell the crew before they startOwners must tell workers and contractors what's there before work that may disturb it.
  • Keep the result with the suite recordA written result per suite builds the register and answers the next turnover in minutes.

This checklist is general guidance, not legal advice. Ontario regulations referenced: O. Reg. 278/05, OHSA section 30 and O. Reg. 490/09.
