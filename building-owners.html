What the law asks of building owners

  • Keep an asbestos record on the premises: where each material is, its condition, and whether it is friable (O. Reg. 278/05).
  • Re-inspect listed materials and update the record at least once every 12 months, and whenever you learn something new.
  • Give occupiers written notice of asbestos in the areas they occupy, and tell contractors before work that may disturb it.
  • Train maintenance workers who work on or near listed materials.
  • Before any renovation, repair or demolition, list every designated substance in the work area and include it in the tender (OHSA s.30).

What's at stake

An owner who leaves a substance off the project list is liable to the constructor and every contractor for their losses when it turns up mid-job. The maximum OHSA fine is $2 million for a corporation and $1.5 million for a director or officer.

In Ontario's 2023–24 inspection campaign, the most common orders were for not following the asbestos management program and not keeping the record on site. Most of the workplaces visited had fewer than 50 workers.

What the program is, and isn't

A designated substance report is triggered by a project: a renovation, a tender, a sale, a demolition. It isn't something you need every year, so we don't bundle it into a monthly fee you'd pay in years without a project.

What you need every year is the record kept current, notices out, staff trained and a straight answer when a contractor asks what's in the ceiling. That's the program. When a project comes, the report costs you 10% to 20% less.
