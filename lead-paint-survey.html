Lead paint testing before renovation and demolition

Lead is one of Ontario's designated substances, and older paint and industrial coatings are where it's usually found. When the work will sand, grind, cut, torch or strip painted surfaces, the contractor needs to know which ones contain lead before pricing the job.

Where lead turns up

  • Paint on trim, doors, windows, radiators and walls in older buildings
  • Primers and coatings on structural steel, tanks, railings and industrial equipment, which kept lead long after house paint stopped
  • Solder, flashing, old water service pipes and some window glazing

How we test and report it

We take paint samples from the surfaces the planned work will disturb and send them to a CALA-accredited lab. Results are reported against the EACO Lead Guideline classes, which tell the contractor what precautions each kind of work needs. The report says which surfaces can be worked on normally and where there must be no sanding, grinding, torch cutting or heat gun without lead precautions.

Lead is part of every designated substance survey we write, alongside asbestos, mercury, silica and the rest of the 11.

Questions people ask

Is there an Ontario regulation for lead on construction projects?

Lead is a designated substance, so it belongs on the section 30 list before a project. The Ministry of Labour's guideline on lead on construction projects, and the EACO Lead Guideline used across the industry, set out how lead work is classified and controlled.

Does paint have to be stripped before demolition?

Not always. What matters is how the work disturbs it. Demolishing painted walls is treated differently from sanding or torch cutting painted steel. The report tells the contractor which work needs which precautions.

Can you test lead without a full survey?

Yes. Tell us which surfaces and what work is planned, and we'll quote the visit and the samples.
