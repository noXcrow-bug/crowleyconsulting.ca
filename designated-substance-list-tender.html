The designated substance list for a tender (OHSA section 30)

For municipalities, school boards, institutions, property managers and the contractors who bid their work. The owner has to know what's in the work area before the project is tendered, and every bidder has to see the same list.

Ask for a quoteBook a call

Written by Noah Crowley, asbestos and designated substances consultant. Last updated November 25, 2026.

What section 30 asks

  • Before a project begins, the owner determines whether any designated substances are present at the project site and prepares a list of them.
  • If the project is tendered, the list goes into the tender documents. Either way, the constructor gets it before a binding contract is signed.
  • The constructor makes sure every prospective contractor and subcontractor receives the list before they're bound by a contract.
  • An owner who leaves a designated substance off the list is liable to the constructor and every contractor for their losses when it turns up.

There's no building-age or project-size threshold. A small repair in a newer building still needs a list; it's just a shorter one.

When to book the survey

Before the drawings go out. A renovation-scope survey takes 15 business days after fieldwork and a full-building survey 15 to 20, including the lab's 10 business days, so book it while the design is being finished. If the job can't wait for standard lab results, rush analysis takes 2–3 business days at double the sample price.

If the scope grows after the survey, the new areas need to be added before that work is tendered.

What bidders need from the report

  • Locations they can find: room names and plans showing where each material is
  • Quantities in metric with imperial beside them, so removal can be priced
  • Friable and non-friable materials kept separate, and presumed materials named as presumed
  • Lead results by class, so the paint work can be priced with the right precautions
  • Areas not accessed named, with materials there that could contain asbestos treated as asbestos-containing until inspected

The report's summary of findings can go into the tender documents as the list. The whole report still has to be given to the constructor.

Questions people ask

Does the list have to cover all 11 designated substances?

Yes. Section 30 is about designated substances, not just asbestos. In most buildings asbestos, lead, mercury and silica are the ones found; the others are considered and noted, usually because of a past industrial use.

Can we reuse an old designated substance report?

Only if it covers the area and the work now planned and still matches the building. Have it reviewed if you're not sure; a gap found during the project costs far more than one found before the tender.

Who has to receive a copy?

The constructor, before a binding contract, and through the constructor every prospective contractor and subcontractor before they're bound. If the project is tendered, the list is part of the tender documents.

What happens if something not on the list turns up?

Work in that area stops except to identify the material, and the Ministry, the owner, the contractor and the joint health and safety committee are notified. The owner is liable for the contractors' losses from a designated substance left off the list.

How much does a survey for a tender cost?

A renovation-scope survey for one work area starts at $1,000, a full-building survey at $1,500, and a pre-demolition survey at $1,750, plus samples at $100 each. All prices are CAD before HST.
