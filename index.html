Questions people ask

What are Ontario's designated substances?

There are 11: acrylonitrile, arsenic, asbestos, benzene, coke oven emissions, ethylene oxide, isocyanates, lead, mercury, silica and vinyl chloride. Asbestos, lead, mercury and silica are the ones usually found in buildings.

How often does a building owner have to update the asbestos record?

At least once every 12 months, and whenever new information comes to light, under O. Reg. 278/05. The record has to be kept on the premises.

Who is responsible for the designated substance list before a renovation?

The project owner. Under section 30 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the owner lists every designated substance in the work area before the project begins and includes the list in the tender.

How much does it cost?

Reports start at $650 for a limited sampling visit and $1,500 for a project-specific designated substance report. Each report is a report fee plus the samples taken, so the total depends on the building. Building programs start at $69 a month per building and the Cleared-to-Start Pass at $199 a month. All prices are CAD before HST.

Does a program include my designated substance report?

No, on purpose. A report is triggered by a project, not the calendar, so you'd be paying for it in years you don't need it. The program keeps your record current and gives you a member rate on every report.

How long do lab results take?

5 business days from when the lab receives the samples. We don't offer rush analysis. If work can't wait, we leave written interim instructions on site.

Do you remove asbestos?

No. We are independent consultants: we survey, write specifications, oversee abatement and test the air, but we never remove or abate anything and take no referral fees.
