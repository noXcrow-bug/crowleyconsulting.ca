Questions people ask

What are Ontario's designated substances?

There are 11: acrylonitrile, arsenic, asbestos, benzene, coke oven emissions, ethylene oxide, isocyanates, lead, mercury, silica and vinyl chloride. Asbestos, lead, mercury and silica are the ones usually found in buildings.

How often does a building owner have to update the asbestos record?

At least once every 12 months, and whenever new information comes to light, under O. Reg. 278/05. The record has to be kept on the premises.

Who is responsible for the designated substance list before a renovation?

The project owner. Under section 30 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the owner lists every designated substance in the work area before the project begins and includes the list in the tender.

How much does it cost?

Designated substance surveys start at $1,000 for one work area and $1,500 for a whole building, plus samples at $100 each. Testing a few materials starts at $450 for the visit, plus samples. The Annual Asbestos Reassessment starts at $1,400 for the first year. All prices are CAD before HST.

Does the Annual Reassessment include my designated substance survey?

No, on purpose. A survey is triggered by a project, not the calendar, so you'd be paying for it in years you don't need it. The reassessment keeps your record current; if you don't have a usable survey yet, a full-building survey comes first.

How long do lab results take?

10 business days from when the lab receives the samples. Rush analysis takes 2–3 business days, at double the sample price. If work can't wait, we leave written interim instructions on site.

Do you remove asbestos?

No. We are independent consultants: we survey, test, write specifications, oversee abatement and test the air, but we never remove or abate anything and take no referral fees.
