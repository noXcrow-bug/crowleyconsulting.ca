Workplace Exposure Program

For employers whose workers use, handle or store a designated substance: collision and auto body shops, countertop fabricators, foundries, concrete products, metal recyclers, plastics and resin plants, labs and hospital sterilization.

Check what applies to youBook a call

What O. Reg. 490/09 asks of employers

  • A written exposure assessment, made with your joint health and safety committee, with a copy to each member. Redo it after any change that could significantly affect exposure.
  • Where workers' health may be affected, a written control program: engineering controls, work and hygiene practices, monitoring, records and training.
  • Keep exposure under the occupational exposure limit.
  • Air monitoring by or under a qualified industrial hygienist, with results posted for 14 days, shared with the JHSC and kept for 5 years.
  • Medical surveillance for asbestos, benzene, coke oven emissions, isocyanates, lead, mercury and silica.
  • Protect other employers' workers at your site.

The regulation doesn't apply on construction projects, where Regulation 833 and the building duties govern.

Who does the monitoring

Exposure monitoring is done by or under our qualified occupational hygienist. Medical surveillance is done by your physician; we coordinate schedules and exposure records.

What inspectors found

The Ministry's 2024–25 campaign on chemical exposure visited 131 workplaces and issued 144 orders and requirements. Inspectors required air monitoring where exposure data was missing and found respiratory protection programs the most common gap.
